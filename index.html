About My Research

My research asks how artificial-intelligence systems that observe and control the physical world can remain reliable when sites, sensors, modalities, clients, and operating conditions differ from those represented during training. I develop spatio-temporal graph learning, multimodal mixture-of-experts, federated learning, and uncertainty- and risk-control methods for heterogeneous cyber-physical systems. My primary application domain is energy, including solar and building-energy forecasting, virtual power plants, and industrial anomaly detection. Across these settings, my objective is not only to reduce average prediction error, but also to determine when a model can act reliably, when it should abstain, and how its failure behavior changes under distribution shift.

My research trajectory follows a coherent progression from physical computation to reliable decision-making. My earlier work on low-power quantum-dot cellular automata, carried out in an information-security laboratory and building on an undergraduate specialization in information security, established a hardware-aware perspective on energy, latency, and computational constraints. My doctoral research then moved from circuit-level constraints to spatio-temporal and multimodal energy modeling using graph neural networks, frequency-aware representations, mixture-of-experts, and federated learning. My emerging independent agenda extends this foundation toward risk-bounded cyber-physical AI: models that quantify uncertainty, detect violated assumptions, and adapt or defer under client, modality, and environmental shift. Thermal sensing and audio or visual deepfake detection serve as out-of-distribution stress-test environments for this common scientific question rather than as disconnected application areas.

Research Program

My work is organized around three connected research thrusts.

  1. 1. Heterogeneous Spatio-Temporal Learning

    I develop graph-based, frequency-aware, and mixture-of-experts models for physical systems whose spatial relationships, temporal dynamics, and sensing conditions vary across sites and forecast horizons.

    Methods: spatio-temporal GNNs, multimodal fusion, temporal patch modeling, frequency-domain learning, and mixture-of-experts.

  2. 2. Distributed and Edge-Aware Intelligence

    I study learning across heterogeneous clients and resource-constrained devices, with particular emphasis on decentralized energy systems and edge–cloud cooperation.

    Methods: federated learning, personalized modeling, client-aware routing, on-device inference, and communication- and compute-aware evaluation.

  3. 3. Risk Calibration and Reliability under Shift

    I investigate how predictive models should respond when exchangeability, calibration, or deployment assumptions fail. The goal is to move from unconstrained predictions to decisions with explicit uncertainty, abstention, and failure monitoring.

    Methods: conformal prediction, selective prediction, drift detection, out-of-distribution evaluation, negative controls, leakage audits, and reproducibility testing.

Public methodological artifact

WaveSeg — Frequency-Boundary Adaptation for Efficient Segmentation

Evidence type: public code, model weights, and interactive demo. Peer-review status: not currently presented as a peer-reviewed publication.

WaveSeg adds a 770-parameter frequency-boundary adapter to SegFormer-B0. On the reported LGG evaluation, HD95 decreased from 29.70 to 22.83 pixels, a 23% reduction. The repository also reports 58.27 FPS at 256 × 256 resolution on an RTX 4060. Patient-level splitting, boundary metrics, ablations, and the negative auxiliary-loss result are documented publicly.

This project supports my broader work on efficient and failure-aware perception; it is not used to claim clinical validation.

Public methodological artifact

CellTriage — Risk-Controlled Battery Quality Decisions

Evidence type: public code and interactive research console. Peer-review status: not currently presented as a peer-reviewed publication.

CellTriage studies the transition from early-cycle battery-life prediction to cost-sensitive accept, reject, or defer decisions. Under the documented cross-validation protocol, the Extra Trees model achieved MAPE 9.57% ± 3.35 and rank correlation ρ = 0.944. The reported 14.2× cost reduction relative to AQL sampling is conditional on the explicitly stated cost matrix.

The central reliability result is a failure result: conformal coverage decreases from approximately 90% in distribution to 60% under held-out campaign shift. This artifact motivates my postdoctoral focus on risk control when exchangeability and deployment assumptions fail.

Public reproducibility artifact

DriftSentinel — Self-Auditing Distribution-Shift Monitoring

Evidence type: public reproducibility and monitoring artifact. Peer-review status: not currently presented as a peer-reviewed publication.

DriftSentinel compares univariate, sequential, and multivariate shift detectors, including PSI, KS and chi-square tests with BH-FDR correction, CUSUM, MMD, and BBSD, on a 101,766-encounter clinical readmission model. Every detector is re-evaluated under 20-seed negative-control settings in which drift is absent by construction. These checks overturn an initial “8/8 critical” result and expose invalid detector behavior. The instrument ships with 93 automated tests, continuous integration, and byte-level determinism checks.

The contribution is the falsifiable monitoring protocol and its reproducibility instrument, not a claim that one detector solves post-deployment monitoring.

Future Research Agenda

From Accurate Forecasts to Risk-Bounded Decisions under Shift

My doctoral research asks how heterogeneous energy systems can be forecast more accurately by modeling spatial structure, multiple sensing modalities, varying forecast horizons, and distributed clients. This work established methods in spatio-temporal graph learning, dynamic multimodal fusion, frequency-aware modeling, and federated mixture-of-experts.

My postdoctoral agenda addresses a different scientific question:

When should a distributed multimodal model act, abstain, adapt, or request additional information when its clients, modalities, or operating environment differ from those represented during training?

The next stage of my research will move beyond average forecast accuracy toward selective and risk-calibrated decision-making. I aim to develop methods that identify violated assumptions, quantify decision risk under shift, and retain reliable behavior when modalities are missing, sensors are corrupted, or clients follow different distributions.

Planned Research Thrusts

  1. Shift-aware multimodal learning

    Develop fusion and expert-routing mechanisms that explicitly model missing, delayed, corrupted, or site-dependent modalities.

  2. Risk-calibrated action and abstention

    Connect uncertainty estimates to operational decisions through selective prediction, conformal risk control, and cost-aware deferral.

  3. Personalized distributed learning

    Study client-aware and communication-efficient methods for heterogeneous cyber-physical sites rather than optimizing only global average performance.

  4. Failure-centered evaluation

    Evaluate worst-site risk, false alarms, detection delay, calibration, risk–coverage behavior, communication cost, latency, and energy use under controlled distribution shifts.

Independence from My Doctoral and Current Grant Work

This agenda is not a larger version of an existing forecasting architecture, Fed-ST-MoE, or VPP-Copilot. My doctoral and current grant work provide the empirical foundation and application context. The postdoctoral contribution is a new learning and decision framework whose scientific endpoint is bounded operational risk under shift, not forecast accuracy or platform deployment alone.

Ongoing Cross-Domain Stress Tests

Thermal and audio sensing are used as methodological stress-test environments for my broader research on distribution-resilient physical AI. They introduce different forms of domain shift—subject and sensor variation in thermal perception, and corpus, codec, resampling, and replay variation in speech anti-spoofing—while preserving a common scientific question: how should a model detect and respond when its deployment distribution differs from its training distribution?

  • ThermalGuard Study in progress
    Exploratory study of identity-minimizing thermal perception for fall-event detection under subject, site, and sensor shift. The evaluation focuses on event-level sensitivity, false alarms, detection delay, and edge-inference constraints. No formal privacy guarantee is claimed.
  • AudioShield Study in progress
    Exploratory cross-corpus anti-spoofing study using WavLM and XLS-R under codec, resampling, replay, and corpus shift. Six controlled experiments and a domain-reliance analysis are documented in the public repository; blind evaluation remains pending, and no final benchmark or state-of-the-art claim is made.
